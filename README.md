# Shiftform

A procedural climbing platformer. Type any seed and you get an endless tower to climb. Every few screens the world crosses into a new biome with new rules and a new look, and every metre of it is proven climbable before you reach it. Height is the score.

## Run

```
npm start
```

Open http://localhost:8080. Zero dependencies, vanilla JS and Canvas.

```
npm test              # unit tests, a couple of seconds
npm run test:generation   # builds and proves whole biomes, a minute or two
npm run test:all
```

## Controls

On phones and tablets, touch buttons appear over the game: ◀ ▶ ▼ on the left, a big ▲ for jump and climb, X dash and F grab on the right, and R, H, B and menu at the top; shop cards and building respond to taps. On a keyboard: arrows or WASD move, Z / Space / Up jump (tap for a hop, hold for a full jump, let go early to cut it short), Up / Down (W / S) climb ladders, ropes and vines (in mid-air with your legs against a wall it kicks you off; alternate walls to chain kicks), X / Shift dash, F grabs, throws and lets go, R returns to your last checkpoint, H shows the solver's route for this stretch, M toggles sound, Esc closes the shop or build mode, and in the climb asks before you leave to the title. A bar under the play area always lists the biome's controls, highlights the ones it adds and lights keys as you press them. The title screen has the full reference.

## Daily Climb, share cards and ghosts

- The Daily Climb is one tower per day, the same for everyone (the seed is the UTC date) until your gear and rewrites reshape it, with 3 lives instead of 5. Endless towers from any seed are still there.
- When you run out of lives you get a share card: the tower, your height, one emoji per biome you climbed through (☄️ marks a comet skip, 💀 where it ended), deaths, the points you earned and your best. Share result copies it (or opens the share sheet on phones).
- Challenge a friend copies a link that carries your whole run. Whoever opens it races your ghost up the same tower: a translucent climber in your biome and a ring on the altitude meter showing how far ahead you are. Runs are stored as compressed inputs (a few hundred characters per minute) and replayed through the same deterministic physics, so the ghost does exactly what you did. Links remember the game version and warn when a ghost was recorded on a different one.

## The climb

- The first biome is always First Steps: no mutators, no hazards, no puzzles, just hops, then a ladder or trampoline, then a slow moving platform, then a crumbling one. The opening banner waits for your first key press.
- Each biome is about four screens tall and has one rule set, one theme and its own puzzles. A spring at its top launches you through a hatch into the next biome; the banner shows the altitude and the new rules.
- The climber has a life of its own: it squashes and wobbles on landings, stretches on take-off, kicks up dust as it runs and skids, and is dressed by the seed until you visit the wardrobe in the little house of the first biome. Its seven rails hold over a hundred pieces in `src/outfit-art.js`: eighteen colours, ten patterns (spots, stripes, checks, zigzags, stars, hearts, tiger stripes and more), twenty-six hats (cowboy, wizard, viking, chef, beret, bowler, halo, propeller, bunny and cat ears, flower crown, pirate, headphones, mohawk, fez, hard hat, grad cap, Santa, sombrero, a space helmet…), ten things that sprout and swing (leaf, antenna, horns, unicorn horn, feather, a bright idea…), fourteen kinds of glasses (hearts, stars, 3D, visor, aviators, cat-eye, eyepatch, goggles, masquerade…), facial hair, freckles, fangs and a bandit bandana, scarves, ties, pearls, medals and leis, and capes, angel, bat and butterfly wings, a backpack, a turtle shell, a quiver or a tail. Surprise me (or R) shuffles a whole outfit. Its eyes follow nearby monsters and items, it beams at pickups and checkpoints, panics in long falls, frowns through dashes and nods off if left alone. Stomps, deaths and perfect comet bounces land with a short hit-stop.
- Roughly half of every climb is platform hops; the rest is ladders, ropes and vines (themed per biome) and trampolines that launch you to high ledges.
- Falling is real: you can drop back down through the whole biome you are in, but never below its floor.
- Every biome's floor has a tunnel mouth at both edges, and a signpost in the biome's own style (weathered wood, riveted steel, neon, carved stone, candy, icicles, vines, a mushroom cap, a paper lantern…) points to one of them: SHOPS. In the first biome a marquee of chasing, colour-cycling bulbs sits on top of it and reads FREE GEAR until you take your free piece. Walk into one and you enter the underground hall, which changes with every biome (a brick underpass, an old mine, a crystal grotto, an aqueduct, a night bazaar, a station, a mushroom hollow, a workshop), a hall with nothing to climb that keeps the biome's rules and gear (mirrored controls stay mirrored; only the flood stays outside), where a merchant (always with an apron and a coin pouch) sells cards, an outfitter (spots and a green flask) sells gear, a rulesmith (stripes and a scroll) rewrites rules, and a shady dice seller in a top hat and shades re-rolls the merchant's cards and the outfitter's gear for 300 points; walk through it and you come out on the other side of the climb. The biome waits while you are inside, and your items come with you. Gear you buy and rules you rewrite down there apply at once to the biome you are in, which is not rebuilt for them: that is the risk you take.
- You have 5 lives. Each death costs one, and crossing a gate gives one back (never above 5). Run out and the climb ends; Enter or R starts the same tower again from the ground.
- Checkpoint flags sit about once a screen. Dying or pressing R puts you back on the last flag you touched, with everything you were carrying.
- Crate Works biomes are built from crate rooms, each a short puzzle with one idea: knock a crate off a shelf and use it as a step, push a steel crate under the exit ledge and stack a wood crate on it, push a steel crate onto a spring so it lands on a high plate that opens a door, or pick the right crate for a water basin with a plate at the bottom (wood floats, steel sinks). Every room is proven solvable and unbeatable without its crates.
- Every biome is built around one focus (Crate Works, Phase Shift, Moving Parts, Crumbling Heights, Swingers, Clockwork, Locksmith, Blade Run, Bouncer with trampolines and bumpers that fling you over gaps too wide to jump, Wind Tunnel with gusts and updraft columns that carry you up, Waterworks with pools in the gaps and flooded shafts to swim up). Its four stretches introduce the idea, develop it, twist it and test it.
- From the second biome on, monsters turn up now and then, more often the higher you climb: patrollers pacing a platform, hoppers leaping from end to end of theirs, flappers circling over a gap, urchins drifting in a slow orbit across one, spiders dropping on a thread from the underside of the platform you are jumping to, and spitters lobbing shots across the jump to their ledge. Touching one kills you; landing on top squashes it and bounces you up (urchins are spiky all round and shots can't be squashed, but shots stop once their spitter is gone). An arrow from the bow kills any of them. Squashed monsters come back if you respawn at an earlier checkpoint.
- Items wait on risky ledges off the route: a jetpack (fly when out of jumps, steering on its own thrust even where you cannot steer in the air), spring boots (five extra air jumps), a shield (survive one hazard), a heart (refill lives) and the rare comet.
- The comet turns every platform one-way: you fly up through them and land on top, bouncing on every landing. Tap jump just as you land (within eight frames either side) and the next bounce goes 35% higher, up to six times your jump; each perfect bounce tightens the window by a frame, down to four. While it lasts the camera leans toward where you are falling so you can see the landing coming. Miss once and the comet burns out on the next landing (the first bounce is free). Squashing a monster counts as a perfect bounce, hazards still kill, and flying out of the top of a biome takes you straight into the next, where the comet ends. Items you hold survive deaths and gates. Skipping them is always safe: the route is proven without them, and each item is proven reachable with a way back.
- Points come from combos and coins. A combo is a chain of jumps that each land higher than the last with no pause in between: the first two links are a run-up, and from the third a counter pops over the climber and each landing pays its link number in points, with a fanfare at 3, 5, 8, 10, 15 and 20. A dashed combo line marks the highest landing that scored, and landings at or below it never count, so points only come from new height. Coins hover above platforms along the route, one point each.
- The world only rolls banes. From the second biome on, each biome brings one to three of them, more as you climb, and every bane you finish a biome under is experience: your level is one plus the banes you have beaten, a level-up card on arrival shows the new ones (or just the rune when nothing was new), and the top bar shows your level and every bane you hold. Hover or tap any rule, card or gear to read what it does.
- Boons are gear, in three tiers: common for 2 runes (Umbrella, Bullet Time, Time Is Yours, Spare Heart for one more life while worn, Lucky Charm for a fifty-fifty chance of a shield at the start of every biome, Grip Boots that take the slip out of Black Ice, and a Torch that doubles how far you see under Lights Out), rare for 3 (Spider, Blink, Moon Shoes, Second Wind) and epic for 4 (Hummingbird, whose three jumps are each nearly full height, Teleport Hop, which now teleports you two and a half jumps up through platforms, and Flywheel), each card and worn piece edged in its tier's colour. The outfitter in the Underpass offers three per biome: in the first biome three commons, and your first piece is free; from then on rares turn up from the second biome and epics from the fifth, more often the higher you climb. You wear up to two at once (top right, next to your runes): a third replaces the oldest, unless you tap one of your worn pieces in the top bar while at the outfitter to give that one up instead, and gear that changes the same physics as one you wear (Second Wind and Hummingbird, Moon Shoes and Umbrella) replaces that one instead; the card shows which piece would go. Gear rests, dimmed, in a biome whose banes it clashes with (Moon Shoes under Lead Boots). Every biome is generated and proven for the banes you face plus the gear you wear when it is built, two biomes ahead of you; platforms are spaced for the banes alone, so gear makes a biome easier, never longer. If a biome cannot be proven with everything, its banes are dropped first, then the gear.
- Stand at the rulesmith and press 1 or 2 (or tap) to cycle one bane of the biome you are in for another bane you have already beaten. Only beaten banes can be swapped out, so every bane has to be climbed once before you are off the hook. Rewriting costs 1 rune, and you earn 1 rune for every biome you finish; changing your pick during the same visit is free, and switching back to the original rule refunds the rune only during that visit.
- From the second biome on, the merchant in the Underpass sells three cards per biome from a deck of twelve, 300 points each. Stand at the stall and the cards pop up over it; press 1, 2 or 3 or tap one to buy, or just walk on. Come back any time while you are in that biome; buy as many as you can afford or keep the points for a biome that needs them more. The deck: jetpack, spring boots, shield, bow (five arrows, V shoots the way you face), heart, comet, an extra heart (one more life, now and on every refill), double points for the biome, and five held tricks that wait in an inventory under your hearts until you press B for build mode (each further B steps to the next item and closes after the last; Tab or a click on a slot also picks one, a click in the world uses it): a plank, a trampoline that launches you five rows, a six-tile ladder, each built wherever you click, a flag that plants a checkpoint where you stand, and an eraser that removes any saw, laser, spikes or monster. Purchases and every trick used are recorded, so ghosts replay them too.
- The altitude meter on the left shows your height, your best for the seed (kept in the browser) and the biome's checkpoints. The sky darkens from haze to night as you climb.

## How it works

- `src/physics.js` is one pure, deterministic step function shared by the game and the solver. Levels carry their own height, so a biome is just a tall level.
- `src/rules.js` holds the mutator catalog. Each mutator is a boon that helps or a bane that makes the climb harder, and `src/mutator-icons.js` gives each its own icon on a green (boon) or red (bane) disc. Banes make up the world (the biome banner and corner label list them) and boons are gear from `src/outfitter.js`. The catalog: moon or lead gravity, short random gravity flips while you run uphill (on running jumps, on the tide, or now and then the whole world), trampoline landings, ice, mirrored controls, double and triple jump, wall jump, all-or-nothing jumps with no air control, dash, time only moves when you move, bullet time in the air, tiny or giant player, glide, darkness, caffeine, teleport hops, rubber walls, fall damage, a flood that starts rising once you climb onto the first ledge, overclocked timers, and Flywheel momentum: keep moving one way for a second and your jumps grow higher and longer until you turn, stop or hit a wall.
- `src/bodies.js` simulates crates (wood, steel, stone) with pushing by mass, stacking (set a crate down against another and it goes on top), lifting and throwing, buoyancy, conveyors, springs, portals, lasers, spikes, switches and pressure plates. Hooks give rope swings.
- `src/generator.js` builds a biome as four segments. Each segment lays a zig-zag climb sized to the real jump, places a puzzle (keys and doors, switches with walls or bridges, portals, crate puzzles, thrown-crate switches, hook swings) and features (moving, phasing, crumbling (they return three seconds after you leave them) and one-way platforms, conveyors, lasers, gates, saws, gravity pads, headwinds, spikes), and ends on a checkpoint or, at the top, the gate. In a Flywheel biome it also lays flat runways before gaps that only a charged jump can cross, and rejects any stretch the charge-disabled solver can still finish. Puzzles unlock gradually over the first biomes.
- `src/solver.js` proves each segment by solving it leg by leg along the generator's route with A* over quantized states using the real physics, backtracking one leg when a leg gets stuck. After every new segment the whole chain is replayed from the biome floor and any segment the new geometry broke is re-solved. Crate and hook puzzles must be unbeatable without their crates or hooks.
- `src/climb.js` holds the run rules: standing on checkpoints, respawning, entering the next biome and altitude. `src/combo.js` scores jump chains, `src/tricks.js` deals the gate cards, and `src/run.js` ties them together with the choose phase and the one-shot plank and eraser acts, which live in the player state so the physics stays pure.
- `src/themes.js` and `src/render.js` give each biome one of thirty-six looks (neon, paper, crystal, ember, frost, void, jungle, dunes, deep sea, blossom, a rainy city skyline, countryside fields with a windmill, a spaceship bulkhead with portholes, candy land, an office behind window blinds, falling matrix code, an inferno of lava falls, a lunar surface under an earthrise, a harbor with sailboats and a lighthouse, a haunted graveyard under a full moon, a synthwave sunset over a neon grid, a smoking factory, a glowing mushroom forest, a sky kingdom of clouds and floating islands, autumn woods, temple ruins, a beehive, a toxic lab, a pirate cove, a stained-glass cathedral, a golden-hour savanna with acacias, giraffes and elephants and a misty bayou of cypress, moss and fireflies, an evening fairground with a lit ferris wheel, a night lantern festival under a full moon, an enchanted library and an old mine) over an altitude-driven sky, with a smoothed camera and the neighbouring biome drawn across gates. `src/backdrops.js` paints the theme backgrounds and tile patterns, `src/player-art.js` the climber, `src/hud.js` the meter, controls bar, inventory, shop and overlays, and `src/icons.js` the item and trick icons.
- `src/sounds.js` turns each pair of physics states into sound events (jumps, landings, dashes, deaths, pickups, plates, flips, springs, the gate launch, portals, hooks, crate lifts, throws, pushes and material-specific landings, crushing). `src/audio.js` synthesizes all of it with the Web Audio API, no samples: effects, proximity loops for saws, lasers, conveyors, platforms, wind and the flood, a per-theme ambience (synth pulses, pencil scratches, glass bells, embers, howling wind, a deep void drone, jungle drips, desert wind, deep-sea bubbles, blossom plucks, distant car horns, birdsong, console beeps, music-box bells, keyboard clicks, digital bleeps, volcanic rumbles, gull cries, ghostly moans, synth arpeggios, factory clanks) that crossfades at gates, and an altitude wind that grows as you climb.
- `src/decor.js` keeps the platforms from looking stamped out: each biome's blocks get seeded chipped corners, pebbles, tufts, bands, lichen patches, cracks and stalactites baked into the tile layer, and a few blocks per screen come alive with eyes that blink and follow you, a bug crawling back and forth along the top, a corner glint or a drip forming and falling underneath.
- `src/life.js` and `src/life-shapes.js` grow each biome's background life from its seed: plants rooted on platform tops behind the tiles, rotating through their theme's kinds so neighbours differ, ground creatures that hop or crawl back and forth along a platform, flyers crossing the screen in parallax depths on glide, wave, loop or swoop paths, and one drifting flock. Each theme has its own family (kelp and jellies, ink ferns and birds, crystal clusters and prism bugs, dead trees and lava worms, pines, hares and owls, nebula flowers and whales, monsteras, frogs and fireflies, cacti, lizards, vultures and tumbleweed, coral, crabs and fish schools, bamboo, blossom trees and petals, streetlamps, hydrants, cats and blimps, sunflowers, wheat, sheep and bees, consoles, drones and robots, lollipops, candy canes and gummy bears, potted plants, water coolers, paper planes and robot vacuums, data pylons, server racks, glitch cubes and data packets, hell spires, bats and ash, satellite dishes, rockets, hopping astronauts and meteors, bollards, anchors, barrels, gulls and crabs, tombstones, pumpkins, ghosts and spiders, palms, arcade cabinets and spinning records, gears and oil drums, toadstools, glowing mushrooms and snails, balloons, pinwheels, kites and hot-air balloons, maples, foxes and crows, broken columns, braziers and bats, wax cells and bee swarms, flasks, drones and roombas, treasure chests, gulls and crabs, candles, statues and pigeons, popcorn carts, striped poles, clown cars and balloon bunches, stone lanterns, bonsai pines, cranes and rising sky lanterns). They never touch play.
- `src/worker.js` generates biomes off the main thread and stays two biomes ahead of you. A biome is keyed by its index, the column it opens at and the rules it is proven for, so changing gear or a rule asks for a fresh chain, and the ghost you race gets its own lane behind yours. While you wait, the generator reports which stretch it is laying or proving and how far along it is, and `src/chatter.js` fills the gap with seeded nonsense about what it is supposedly busy with.

The seed lives in the URL hash, so a link reproduces the same tower.
