# Shiftform

A procedural climbing platformer. Type any seed and you get an endless tower to climb. Every few screens the world crosses into a new biome with new rules and a new look, and every metre of it is proven climbable before you reach it. Height is the score.

## Run

```
npm start
```

Open http://localhost:8080. Zero dependencies, vanilla JS and Canvas.

```
npm test              # unit tests, a couple of seconds
npm run test:generation   # builds and proves whole biomes, about ten minutes
npm run test:all
```

## Build a level

Press **Build a level** on the title screen. You hover over the tower: arrows or WASD fly, the mouse wheel scrolls. Pick a block from the panel, click to place it, and drag to stretch blocks that stretch. Right-click or Erase removes things. Wire connects plates, keys, switches and timers to doors, shutters and bridges (a new one wires itself to the last free one). Level sets the name, look, height and rules. P or Play tests it from the start, and reaching the goal brings you back. Your work saves itself in this browser.

Share copies a link that holds the whole level, with no server involved. It is usually under a thousand characters, and a level can hold up to 600 items. Whoever opens it sees a small map of the level and can play it or remix it. Remixing asks before it replaces a level you have saved. Nothing checks that a level can be beaten: that is up to its builder.

## Controls

On phones and tablets, touch buttons appear over the game: ◀ ▶ ▼ on the left, a big ▲ for jump and climb, X dash, F grab (and T teleport with Teleport Hop) on the right, and R, H, B and menu at the top; shop cards and building respond to taps. On a keyboard: arrows or WASD move, Z / Space / Up jump (tap for a hop, hold for a full jump, let go early to cut it short), Up / Down (W / S) climb ladders, ropes and vines (in mid-air with your legs against a wall it kicks you off; alternate walls to chain kicks; marked walls, the ones with claw scratches on their face, let anyone wall-jump off them outright, and two facing marked walls climb a shaft), hold X / Shift to dash and let go to stop it, on a rope Up and Down reel it in and out, which also speeds up or slows down your swing, left and right push the swing (press with it to build height, against it to brake), Z or Space jumps off and F lets go, keeping your speed, T teleports with Teleport Hop, F grabs (any crate within about a dozen pixels of you, a step up or down, bouncing or afloat), throws and lets go, R returns to your last checkpoint (only with your feet on the ground and standing still, or on a ladder, rope or in water: never in mid-air, so a bad jump cannot be undone), H shows the solver's route for this stretch, M toggles sound, Esc closes the shop or build mode, and in the climb opens a prompt with Stay, Restart level (R, puts you back on this biome's floor with its flags and the architect's clock cleared, no life lost, so you can race it again) and Leave. A bar under the play area always lists the biome's controls, highlights the ones it adds and lights keys as you press them. The title screen has the full reference. Every key press counts even if it is shorter than a frame. Running starts gently, so a tap moves you a little and holding builds to full speed in 15 frames (250 ms); pushing the other way on the ground brakes several times as hard, and that grip lasts through a turn: change direction within a quarter of a second of running (a gap between the keys is fine) and you are back up to running speed the other way in a few frames, while a turn after a longer pause starts from scratch; and reversing at a run skids: the climber throws its weight hard into the turn, pivoting on its feet and tipping further the faster it was going (about 36 degrees from a full run), and kicks up dust. Let go of the keys at a walk and the climber stops at the lip of a ledge instead of sliding off, teetering over the drop before it pulls back (a run carries you over, and Black Ice keeps its slide). Hazards forgive a two-pixel graze, and one jump spends the ground grace, so a quick double tap is never a hidden second jump. The edges of the screen give one kick but are not walls for Spider, and a kick off a wall never flips gravity. Walking off a ledge gives a few frames of grace to jump, not a spare jump for the whole fall; only gear such as Second Wind adds jumps in mid-air, while trampolines, bumpers, stomps and water hand you a fresh jump.

## Daily Climb, share cards and ghosts

- Your best climbs: the title screen keeps a personal leaderboard of your best height on every tower you have climbed, top fifty, highest first, stored in the browser. Tap one to climb that tower again (past Daily Climbs replay with Daily rules).
- The Daily Climb is one tower per day, the same for everyone (the seed is the UTC date) no matter which skills you buy or difficulty you choose. Endless towers from any seed are still there.
- When you run out of lives you get a share card: the tower, your height, one emoji per biome you climbed through (☄️ marks a comet skip, 💀 where it ended), deaths, the points you earned and your best. Share result copies it (or opens the share sheet on phones).
- Challenge a friend copies a link that carries your whole run. Whoever opens it races your ghost up the same tower: a translucent climber in your biome and a ring on the altitude meter showing how far ahead you are. Runs are stored as compressed inputs (a few hundred characters per minute) and replayed through the same deterministic physics, so the ghost does exactly what you did. Links remember the game version and warn when a ghost was recorded on a different one.

## The climb

- The first biome is always First Steps: no mutators, no hazards, no puzzles, just hops, then a ladder or trampoline, then a slow moving platform, then a crumbling one. The opening banner waits for your first key press.
- Each biome is about four screens tall and has one rule set, one theme and its own puzzles. A spring at its top launches you through a hatch into the next biome; the banner shows the altitude and the new rules.
- Everything is lit from the upper left, like the monsters: platforms cast a soft shadow onto the scene and darken towards their undersides, moving and switching parts share a shaded body with a small highlight (`slab`, `contactShadow` and `glint` in `src/paint.js`), and hills and ridges get a lit edge that fades into haze. `gallery.html?section=elements` shows every element per theme.
- Water can be painted square by square in the builder, and the game works out where the surface is. In water the climber swims with jump, never spins, and can only poke a little past the surface, enough to climb onto a ledge but not to leap out; jumps are spent on the way out, so springs and jetpacks are the only strong ways up. About one biome in three or four, from level 2, floods one stretch: a band of rock with a winding tunnel of water that sweeps across the screen, up and down, with teeth to weave between, spikes, eels, jellyfish, currents that push you back and side pockets holding coins, all proven like any other stretch (`src/sunken.js`). They grow with the level: a second sweep from level 4, denser dangers and stronger odds of currents, and if a fierce layout cannot be proven the generator tries a gentler one.
- The climber has a life of its own: it squashes and wobbles on landings and head bumps, harder the harder it hits, squeezes thin against a wall it hits or kicks off, stretches on take-off, kicks up dust as it runs and skids, and is dressed by the seed until you visit the wardrobe in the little house of the first biome. Its seven rails hold over a hundred pieces in `src/outfit-art.js`: eighteen colours, ten patterns (spots, stripes, checks, zigzags, stars, hearts, tiger stripes and more), twenty-six hats (cowboy, wizard, viking, chef, beret, bowler, halo, propeller, bunny and cat ears, flower crown, pirate, headphones, mohawk, fez, hard hat, grad cap, Santa, sombrero, a space helmet…), ten things that sprout and swing (leaf, antenna, horns, unicorn horn, feather, a bright idea…), fourteen kinds of glasses (hearts, stars, 3D, visor, aviators, cat-eye, eyepatch, goggles, masquerade…), facial hair, freckles, fangs and a bandit bandana, scarves, ties, pearls, medals and leis, and capes, angel, bat and butterfly wings, a backpack, a turtle shell, a quiver or a tail. Surprise me (or R) shuffles a whole outfit. Its eyes follow nearby monsters and items, it beams at pickups and checkpoints, panics in long falls, frowns through dashes and nods off if left alone. Stomps, deaths and perfect comet bounces land with a short hit-stop.
- Every biome leaves its floor clear: the first move up is a ladder, rope, vine or trampoline that lifts you several rows before any platform (in the first biome it stands just outside the house), so the tunnels, signposts and the house are not buried in ledges. Only biomes whose gravity flips, where ladders and trampolines are never used, start with hops.
- Roughly half of every climb is platform hops; the rest is ladders, ropes and vines (themed per biome) and trampolines that launch you to high ledges.
- Falling is real: you can drop back down through the whole biome you are in, but never below its floor.
- Every biome's floor has a tunnel mouth at both edges, and a signpost in the biome's own style (weathered wood, riveted steel, neon, carved stone, candy, icicles, vines, a mushroom cap, a paper lantern…) points to one of them: SHOPS. In the first biome a marquee of chasing, colour-cycling bulbs sits on top of it and reads FREE SKILL until you take your free level. Beside the house in the first biome a second signpost, the same board tilted sixty degrees up toward the ladder or trampoline, says CLIMB; it stands in the first clear spot, never over the way up. Walk into one and you enter the underground hall, which changes with every biome (a brick underpass, an old mine, a crystal grotto, an aqueduct, a night bazaar, a station, a mushroom hollow, a workshop), a hall with nothing to climb that keeps the biome's rules and your skills (mirrored controls stay mirrored; only the flood and your teleports stay outside), where a merchant sells cards from the second biome on, a trainer sells skill levels, and a shady dice seller in a top hat and shades re-rolls the merchant's cards and the trainer's skills for 300 gold; walk through it and you come out on the other side of the climb. The biome waits while you are inside, and your items come with you. Gear you buy and rules you rewrite down there apply at once to the biome you are in, which is not rebuilt for them: that is the risk you take.
- You always start with 3 lives, on the Daily Climb too. Each death costs one, and crossing a gate gives one back (never above 3 plus any Heart skill levels). Run out and the climb ends; Enter or R starts the same tower again from the ground.
- Checkpoint flags sit about once a screen. Dying or pressing R puts you back on the highest flag you have touched, with everything you were carrying. Every death, except one that would leave it in a laser beam, also drops a small tombstone from where you died; it falls until it lands on something and stays for the rest of the biome as a one-way ledge you can stand on next time (deaths that fall out of the biome leave nothing); die at a trampoline and the stone is catapulted off in a random direction, spinning, and stays wherever it comes down). Every death leaves an echo: the last second and a half of your path stays on screen as a line in your own colour with a cross where you died, fading over a couple of seconds, so you can see the mistake without being told.
- Every biome ships with the proof that it can be climbed, and that proof climbs beside you. Once you are ten metres above the biome's floor (so the walk to the shops and back is free), a dashed outline in the biome's accent colour sets off from the floor and follows the proof's exact path at the proof's own pace, and a hollow diamond on the altitude meter shows where it is when it is off screen. It climbs through your deaths and only waits while you are in the Underpass or between biomes. Overtaking it bursts a few sparks, and reaching the gate no later than it did is a PERFECT: the caption alone, fireworks, and the biome's runes and gold doubled on the biome-cleared banner, with no mention of the architect anywhere in the game.
- Speed carries: jump off a moving platform or conveyor and you keep its speed, and handles and cannons fling you faster than you can run for as long as you hold that direction, until you land. F takes any handle in reach, even from the ground, and lets it go at its current speed; jumping off adds your jump to it. Z fires a cannon, and some fire themselves.
- From the second biome the climb turns on you, and by the fifth (level 4) it is brutal: narrow ledges, longer gaps, no gentle stretches and killers everywhere, and it keeps getting worse above that. Each biome casts its own set of killers from saws, mines, pendulums, crushers, flame jets, lasers, turrets, flappers, urchins and spiders, and its theme decides what its static hazards look like: blades, shards, thorns, lava, ooze or electric arcs. The floor of a biome and the air just above it stay empty so the shops can always be reached, and the top ledge is always clean.
- Puzzle rooms are switched off for now (`PUZZLE_ROOMS` in `src/focus.js`); the code stays and asking for a riddle focus by name still builds them. When on: Puzzles are chambers: at most one per biome, and none if no room good enough turns up. One sealed, screen-sized room between two stretches, entered through a hatch in its floor and left through its roof, with the camera holding the whole room in view. Each is planned as logic first, so no two need the same thinking: a crate can be a weight on a plate, a shield carried under a ceiling laser, a plug on a floor emitter, a stepping stone thrown onto spikes, or something dropped down a shaft or sent through a portal; plates and switches can open one door while they shut another. Levers flip both ways, and a lock can sit behind a small gate that combines two signals, so a door may want both, either, exactly one, or one on and the other off. Wires in matching colours run from each plate, key, switch, lever and timer to what it drives. R always resets the room.
- Every biome is built around one focus (Crate Works, Live Wires, Phase Shift, Moving Parts, Crumbling Heights, Swingers, Clockwork, Locksmith, Blade Run, Cold Light, Stepping Stones, Wormholes, The Lab, Momentum with pendulums, rail handles and spinning wheels you grab and ride, Cannonade with barrels that fire you across the gap, Bouncer with trampolines and bumpers that fling you over gaps too wide to jump, Wind Tunnel with gusts and updraft columns that carry you up, Waterworks with pools in the gaps and flooded shafts to swim up, Wallrun with marked-wall shafts, Crystal Climb with rings that give back a jump when you fly through them (a ring stays lit until you spend that jump, then rests until you next land), and Stopwatch with switches that hold a door open for a few seconds). Its four stretches introduce the idea, develop it, twist it and test it.
- From the second biome on, monsters turn up now and then, more often the higher you climb: patrollers pacing a platform, hoppers leaping from end to end of theirs, flappers circling over a gap, urchins drifting in a slow orbit across one, spiders dropping on a thread from the underside of the platform you are jumping to, and spitters lobbing shots across the jump to their ledge. Touching one kills you; landing on top squashes it and bounces you up (urchins are spiky all round and shots can't be squashed, but shots stop once their spitter is gone). An arrow from the bow kills any of them. Squashed monsters come back if you respawn at an earlier checkpoint.
- Others react to you. A faller clings to a ceiling with its eyes shut, shakes when you pass beneath and drops; a hound wakes when it sees you and runs you down along its ledge without ever running off the edge; a swooper hangs overhead, locks onto where you are and dives there, so stepping aside dodges it; a snapper is two eyes on a ledge until you come close and its jaws come up; a bomber waddles after you, lights its fuse and blows, unless you stomp it first; a charger paws the ground, then runs faster than you can until it hits a wall and stands stunned; a sniper's eye tracks you along a dashed line and fires where it locked; and a wraith floats through walls after you, freezing and hiding its face while you look at it. Crawlers walk round the edge of a ledge, puffers swell and shrink on a timer and leapers jump up out of the gaps. Reactive monsters forget you on the floor, walk home when you get away and go back to their posts when you respawn or touch a flag. `src/lurkers.js` holds their behaviour; `src/gauntlet.js` only keeps one if the whole proof replays clear of it.
- Items wait on risky ledges off the route: a jetpack (fly when out of jumps, steering on its own thrust even where you cannot steer in the air), spring boots (five extra air jumps), a shield (survive one hazard), a heart (refill lives) and the rare comet.
- The comet turns every platform one-way: you fly up through them and land on top, bouncing on every landing. Tap jump just as you land (within eight frames either side) and the next bounce goes 35% higher, up to six times your jump; each perfect bounce tightens the window by a frame, down to four. While it lasts the camera leans toward where you are falling so you can see the landing coming. Miss once and the comet burns out on the next landing (the first bounce is free). Squashing a monster counts as a perfect bounce, hazards still kill, and flying out of the top of a biome takes you straight into the next, where the comet ends. Items you hold survive deaths and gates. Skipping them is always safe: the route is proven without them, and each item is proven reachable with a way back.
- Points come from combos and coins. A combo is a chain of jumps that each land higher than the last with no pause in between: the first two links are a run-up, and from the third a counter pops over the climber and each landing pays its link number in points, with a fanfare at 3, 5, 8, 10, 15 and 20. A dashed combo line marks the highest landing that scored, and landings at or below it never count, so points only come from new height. A second dashed line marks your best height on this tower when it falls inside the biome you are in. Reach a combo and your next jump is a long jump: it carries a quarter further, at the same height, with its own sound and a spray from under your feet, and you keep long jumping for as long as each one lands higher without a pause. The jump sound climbs with the chain on the way there. The proof never uses it, because a climb can't count on arriving with a combo. Coins hover above platforms along the route, one point each.
- You choose how hard the climb is. A red imp with a pitchfork stands on every biome's floor: stand by it and press 1 to 4 (or tap a card). Normal has no banes and all three checkpoint flags and pays 1 rune and 100 gold for finishing a biome; Hard adds one bane, takes one flag and pays 2 runes and 200 gold; Torment adds two banes, takes two flags and pays 3 runes and 400 gold; Impossible adds three banes, takes every flag and pays 5 runes and 600 gold. Which banes and flags is fixed by the seed and biome, and each tier adds to the one below. The choice carries up the tower until you change it. Changing it costs no life but wipes the biome you are in: your flags, the architect's clock, your tombstones, every lever and monster, and you stay where you are at the imp; coins and items already taken stay taken, so switching cannot farm them. Every bane you finish a biome under is experience: your level is one plus the banes you have beaten, and the top bar shows them. Hover or tap any rule, card or skill to read what it does.
- Skills are what you spend runes on, at the trainer in the Underpass (the SKILLS stall). Press 1 to 6 or tap a card. There are thirteen, each up to level 5, and they last the whole climb. The trainer offers six of them per biome, picked at random by the seed and never one you have maxed, and the dice seller can re-roll them: Torch (brighter under Lights Out), Jump (one more air jump per level, each 10% weaker than the last), Spider (wall jumps: one more kick off the same wall per level, landing or another wall starts the count again), Jumper (every jump about 8% higher per level), Dash (level 1 gives the dash, 88 px, and each level adds 33 px), Teleport (level + 1 charges per biome, recharged by the next one and topped up when you buy a level), Shield (one more shield per level: each stops one hit and is gone for the rest of the biome, a hit also drops the remaining shields for seven seconds, deaths do not refill them and a new biome does; bought or found shields are spent after these), Umbrella (float while holding jump, slower each level), Flywheel (charge power from 60% to 140%), Jetpack (fills your tank when bought, and every jetpack holds 1.2x fuel at level 1, up to 2x at level 5), Bow (fills your quiver when bought, every bow holds 7 arrows at level 1 up to 15 at level 5, and one arrow's shot flies more ways: backwards too at level 1, then up, then both upward diagonals, both downward diagonals and finally straight down), Heart (one more life per level) and Lucky Charm (20% per level for a shield at the start of each biome). A level costs its own number in runes, 1 for level 1 up to 5 for level 5, and your very first level in the first biome is free. Marked walls stay free for everyone. Skills go on top of a biome and never change how it is built, so buying never makes you wait.
- The dice seller re-rolls the merchant's cards and the trainer's skills for 300 gold.
- From the second biome on, the merchant in the Underpass sells three cards per biome from a deck of twelve, 300 points each. Stand at the stall and the cards pop up over it; press 1, 2 or 3 or tap one to buy, or just walk on. Come back any time while you are in that biome; buy as many as you can afford or keep the points for a biome that needs them more. The deck: jetpack, spring boots, shield, bow (five arrows, V shoots the way you face), heart, comet, an extra heart (one more life, now and on every refill), double points for the biome, and five held tricks that wait in an inventory under your hearts until you press B for build mode (each further B steps to the next item and closes after the last; Tab or a click on a slot also picks one, a click in the world uses it): a plank, a trampoline that launches you five rows, a six-tile ladder, each built wherever you click, a flag that plants a checkpoint where you stand, and an eraser that removes any saw, laser, spikes or monster. Purchases and every trick used are recorded, so ghosts replay them too.
- The altitude meter on the left shows your height, your best for the seed (kept in the browser) and the biome's checkpoints. The sky darkens from haze to night as you climb.

## Testing locally

When the game runs from a checkout (`npm start`, so `src/version.js` still says `dev`), a golden god merchant with a halo and wings floats on the floor of every biome, opposite the SHOPS sign. Stand under him and press 1–7 or tap a card: 10 runes, 5,000 points, one more life, every item and three of every buildable trick, every bane collected, a lift to the top platform just under the exit, or More, which pages through every skill to level for free (it goes on at once). Deployed builds stamp a real version into `src/version.js`, so he never appears on the live site.


## How it works

- `src/physics.js` is one pure, deterministic step function shared by the game and the solver. Levels carry their own height, so a biome is just a tall level.
- `src/rules.js` holds the mutator catalog. Each mutator is a boon that helps or a bane that makes the climb harder, and `src/mutator-icons.js` gives each its own icon on a green (boon) or red (bane) disc. Banes make up the world (the biome banner and corner label list them, and `src/difficulty.js` says how many a biome gets) and boons remain for the generator's tests; what players buy are the levelled skills in `src/skills.js`, applied over the rules once a biome is built. The catalog: moon or lead gravity, a running jump that can flip gravity for a moment (Flip Jumps, the only bane that turns gravity around), trampoline landings, ice, mirrored controls, double and triple jump, wall jump, all-or-nothing jumps with no air control, dash, time only moves when you move, bullet time in the air, tiny or giant player, glide, darkness, caffeine, teleport hops, rubber walls, fall damage, the Pursuit (once you climb onto the first ledge the biome rises after you: water, ash and embers, freezing fog, a devouring void, overgrowth or a sandstorm depending on the theme, and its pace comes from the proof: `src/pursuit.js` turns the proof's own climb into a surface that always stays most of a tile under where the proof will stand next, run at two and a half times the proof's time with a three-second head start and never faster than 2.5 pixels a frame, so the proof and any climber at that pace survive while a slower one is swallowed, and it keeps rising after the proof's end, and it starts creeping up from just below the floor the moment the tide wakes, at least 0.4 pixels a frame whenever the proof is climbing, approaching each step of the proof at a steady pace instead of lurching), overclocked timers, and Flywheel momentum: keep moving one way for a second and your jumps grow higher and longer until you turn, stop or hit a wall.
- `src/bodies.js` simulates crates (wood, steel, stone) with pushing by mass (a floating crate weighs nothing, so a heavier crate shoves it aside; trampolines and bumpers bend and flash under crates just as they do under the climber, and a floating crate bobs on a slow swell, rocks with it, sinks deeper under the climber's weight and carries them up and down while they stand on it), stacking (set a crate down against another and it goes on top), lifting and throwing, buoyancy, conveyors, springs, portals, lasers, spikes, switches and pressure plates. Hooks give rope swings.
- `src/generator.js` builds a biome as four stretches. For each stretch `src/moves.js` writes a script of moves from the focus's vocabulary within an action budget that grows with depth (hops, stairs, leaps, long runs, ladder and trampoline shafts, drops, forks where a single risky leap skips a safe pair of hops, soaring gaps crossed with a ring that refills a jump mid-air, rigs from `src/rigs.js` where a gap too wide to jump is crossed by a handle or a cannon, and turns that climb a shaft back the other way, plus the wide gaps a traversal puzzle reserves), lays several candidate layouts from it and `src/critics.js` keeps the one most unlike the stretches you have just climbed that still fits the focus; the worker hands each biome's stretch profiles to the next so the climb does not repeat itself across biomes. Then it places the stage's traversal puzzle (portals, bumpers, hook swings) and features (moving, phasing, crumbling (they return three seconds after you leave them) and one-way platforms, conveyors, lasers, gates, saws, gravity pads, headwinds, spikes), and ends on a checkpoint or, at the top, the gate. `src/gauntlet.js` dresses the proven climb last and never touches the proof: for every jump in it, it simulates pressing earlier and later and holding shorter and longer, keeps each variant that still lands on the same footing, and treats all those paths as a protected tube, so a hazard can only go where it misses the tube. The tolerance (seven frames either way on the second biome, down to two from the seventh, never frame perfect) is the difficulty knob, alongside how many killers each gap gets and how much spare ledge is carpeted. Moving hazards may never sweep a spot the proof stands on, and they get a phase that clears the proof at every frame, so waiting on a ledge is always safe and the window always comes round again. `src/riddle.js` is the puzzle tier: a room is cells joined by locks (doors, shutters, beams, plugs, pits, bridges, portals, fuses, ladders, hatches, springs, drops), and a breadth-first search over player cell, carried crate, crate positions and flags solves it exactly in microseconds. `src/chamber.js` composes rooms and tunes their wiring toward conflict until enough clear a hard bar (something must be undone and, from the fourth biome on, something undone or shut twice over, and every part must be needed), scores them from the solution (work, reuse of a crate, backtracking, having to undo a lever or plate, moves that shut a lock you still need, moves that doom the room, parts that are needed, how unlike recent chambers it is, the focus's star mechanic, a target that grows with depth), then lays the best as tiles and objects and hands the solver a route from the logical solution. Each room is laid out sixteen ways and the one that makes you walk back on yourself and return to decks wins; a room that is a straight walk is thrown away. Keys and switches can sit on shelves you must jump to, and floors can carry spikes to hop. A proof failure moves on to the next room, and a room with a path around its mechanisms is thrown away. `npm run puzzles` measures what proofs actually do: signatures, entropy, repeats inside a climb and, with `--knockouts on`, whether every mechanism is needed. `npm run range` measures the generator's expressive range (rise, turns, linearity, jump cadence, hazards and the mechanics each proof fires) over many seeds. In a Flywheel biome it also lays flat runways before gaps that only a charged jump can cross, and rejects any stretch the charge-disabled solver can still finish. Puzzles unlock gradually over the first biomes.
- `src/blueprint.js` is the level format and block catalogue: a blueprint is plain data (tiles, placed blocks, wires) that builds a real level and packs into a link, and links are validated when they are opened. `src/editor.js` edits blueprints with undo and auto-wiring, and `src/editor-ui.js` is the build panel.
- `src/structures.js` reads each finished biome and works out what holds it up: masts tied together with walled or braced bays, posts with forked heads, diagonal struts, trestles, hangers and rails under moving platforms, with a few ledges left floating. It is purely visual and never touches physics or proofs. `src/structure-art.js` bakes that plan behind the tiles in three hazed layers from a per-theme kit (`src/structure-kits.js`) of materials, bays and ornaments, so the 36 themes each build differently. See it on real biomes in `gallery.html?section=structures`.
- `src/solver.js` proves each segment by solving it leg by leg along the generator's route with A* over quantized states using the real physics, backtracking one leg when a leg gets stuck. After every new segment the whole chain is replayed from the biome floor and any segment the new geometry broke is re-solved. Chambers are searched for a way through without their mechanisms; a hook may have a harder way around it.
- `src/climb.js` holds the run rules: standing on checkpoints, respawning, entering the next biome and altitude. `src/combo.js` scores jump chains, `src/tricks.js` deals the gate cards, and `src/run.js` ties them together with the choose phase and the one-shot plank and eraser acts, which live in the player state so the physics stays pure.
- `src/themes.js` and `src/render.js` give each biome one of thirty-six looks (neon, paper, crystal, ember, frost, void, jungle, dunes, deep sea, blossom, a rainy city skyline, countryside fields with a windmill, a spaceship bulkhead with portholes, candy land, an office behind window blinds, falling matrix code, an inferno of lava falls, a lunar surface under an earthrise, a harbor with sailboats and a lighthouse, a haunted graveyard under a full moon, a synthwave sunset over a neon grid, a smoking factory, a glowing mushroom forest, a sky kingdom of clouds and floating islands, autumn woods, temple ruins, a beehive, a toxic lab, a pirate cove, a stained-glass cathedral, a golden-hour savanna with acacias, giraffes and elephants and a misty bayou of cypress, moss and fireflies, an evening fairground with a lit ferris wheel, a night lantern festival under a full moon, an enchanted library and an old mine) over an altitude-driven sky, with a smoothed camera and the neighbouring biome drawn across gates. `src/backdrops.js` paints the theme backgrounds and tile patterns, `src/player-art.js` the climber, `src/hud.js` the meter, controls bar, inventory, shop and overlays, and `src/icons.js` the item and trick icons.
- `src/sounds.js` turns each pair of physics states into sound events (jumps, landings, dashes, deaths, pickups, plates, flips, springs, the gate launch, portals, hooks, crate lifts, throws, pushes and material-specific landings, crushing). `src/audio.js` synthesizes all of it with the Web Audio API: one-shot effects, plus a rumble that tells you how close the flood is. `src/music.js` streams the soundtrack: five moods shared across the themes and one for the Underpass, one track at a time, fading in after five seconds, resting between tracks and picking up where it left off when you come back from the shops.
- `src/decor.js` keeps the platforms from looking stamped out: each biome's blocks get seeded chipped corners, pebbles, tufts, bands, lichen patches, cracks and stalactites baked into the tile layer, and a few blocks per screen come alive with eyes that blink and follow you, a bug crawling back and forth along the top, a corner glint or a drip forming and falling underneath.
- `src/life.js` and `src/life-shapes.js` grow each biome's background life from its seed. Every plant is a list of parts drawn in palette slots (each theme's palette derives deep and light tones of its accent and edge colours, so an asset can carry a base, a hard shadow and a highlight without per-theme colour work), with optional coloured outlines and a baked glow in glowing themes; each plant is rasterised once into a sprite that sways as a whole, so the extra layers cost nothing per frame. Plants rooted on platform tops behind the tiles, rotating through their theme's kinds so neighbours differ, small ground creatures that hop or crawl back and forth along a platform, none taller than half the climber, drawn faded in their own natural colours with a soft shadow so they read as scenery, not as something to touch; the bigger ones (hare, cat, fox, bird, owl) walk on little legs that stride with their pace and tuck up mid-hop, and eyes carry a highlight once they are big enough to show it, flyers crossing the screen in parallax depths on glide, wave, loop or swoop paths, and one drifting flock. Each theme has its own family (kelp and jellies, ink ferns and birds, crystal clusters and prism bugs, dead trees and lava worms, pines, hares and owls, nebula flowers and whales, monsteras, frogs and fireflies, cacti, lizards, vultures and tumbleweed, coral, crabs and fish schools, bamboo, blossom trees and petals, streetlamps, hydrants, cats and blimps, sunflowers, wheat, sheep and bees, consoles, drones and robots, lollipops, candy canes and gummy bears, potted plants, water coolers, paper planes and robot vacuums, data pylons, server racks, glitch cubes and data packets, hell spires, bats and ash, satellite dishes, rockets, hopping astronauts and meteors, bollards, anchors, barrels, gulls and crabs, tombstones, pumpkins, ghosts and spiders, palms, arcade cabinets and spinning records, gears and oil drums, toadstools, glowing mushrooms and snails, balloons, pinwheels, kites and hot-air balloons, maples, foxes and crows, broken columns, braziers and bats, wax cells and bee swarms, flasks, drones and roombas, treasure chests, gulls and crabs, candles, statues and pigeons, popcorn carts, striped poles, clown cars and balloon bunches, stone lanterns, bonsai pines, cranes and rising sky lanterns). They never touch play.
- `src/worker.js` generates biomes off the main thread and stays two biomes ahead of you. A biome is always proven with no banes and no skills, so difficulty and purchases never ask for a rebuild; they are laid over the finished biome, which is why a harder setting is not itself proven. The ghost you race gets its own lane behind yours. While you wait, the generator reports which stretch it is laying or proving and how far along it is, and `src/chatter.js` fills the gap with seeded nonsense about what it is supposedly busy with.

The seed lives in the URL hash, so a link reproduces the same tower.
